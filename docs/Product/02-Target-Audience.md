# Target Audience

> "Every person looks at the same sky, but everyone sees something different."

---

# Purpose

AstroAlert is designed for people, not demographics.

Instead of defining users only by age or profession, this document describes the different kinds of people who may use AstroAlert, their motivations, their challenges and the experiences they expect.

Every feature should solve a real need for at least one of these users.

---

# Our Audience

AstroAlert welcomes anyone who is curious about the universe.

Some users arrive because they want to learn.

Others because they want to observe.

Some simply want to know what is happening in the sky tonight.

No matter their experience level, AstroAlert should always feel approachable, inspiring and trustworthy.

---

# User Persona 01

## The Curious Beginner

### Description

Someone who has recently discovered an interest in astronomy.

They may have watched a documentary, seen an eclipse or simply looked up at the night sky and wanted to know more.

### Goals

- Learn astronomy without feeling overwhelmed.
- Understand celestial events.
- Discover planets and constellations.
- Build curiosity.

### Challenges

- Scientific terminology can be intimidating.
- Doesn't know where to start.
- Finds many astronomy websites too technical.

### AstroAlert Helps By

- Using simple explanations.
- Providing visual experiences.
- Organizing information clearly.
- Encouraging exploration instead of memorization.

---

# User Persona 02

## The Amateur Observer

### Description

Someone who enjoys observing the sky regularly.

They may own binoculars or a telescope.

They like checking astronomical events before going outside.

### Goals

- Know which planets are visible.
- Track eclipses.
- Observe conjunctions.
- Follow comets.
- Plan observation sessions.

### Challenges

- Information is often scattered across multiple websites.
- Event schedules are sometimes difficult to understand.

### AstroAlert Helps By

- Centralizing astronomical information.
- Providing real-time visibility data.
- Offering observation tools.
- Simplifying event planning.

---

# User Persona 03

## The Student

### Description

A student looking for reliable astronomy information for school, university or personal learning.

### Goals

- Learn scientific concepts.
- Complete academic projects.
- Understand astronomical events.
- Access trustworthy information.

### Challenges

- Information online can be confusing.
- Educational resources are often outdated or difficult to understand.

### AstroAlert Helps By

- Presenting reliable information.
- Explaining concepts clearly.
- Combining education with interaction.
- Making learning enjoyable.

# Shared Expectations

Regardless of who they are, every AstroAlert user expects:

- Accurate scientific information.
- A clean and elegant interface.
- Fast access to important events.
- Beautiful visual experiences.
- Easy navigation.
- Reliable data.
- Continuous discovery.

---

# What Users Should Feel

When using AstroAlert, users should feel:

- Curious.
- Inspired.
- Calm.
- Confident.
- Motivated to learn.
- Amazed by the universe.

They should never feel:

- Lost.
- Overwhelmed.
- Confused.
- Distracted.
- Intimidated.

---

# Design Implications

Every design decision should answer these questions:

- Is this understandable for a beginner?
- Is this useful for an experienced observer?
- Does this encourage curiosity?
- Is the scientific information trustworthy?
- Does the interface make exploration easier?

If the answer is "no", the feature should be redesigned.

---

# Future Audience

As AstroAlert evolves, it should also support:

- Families exploring astronomy together.
- Citizen scientists.
- Amateur astrophotographers.
- Astronomy clubs.
- Schools and universities.
- Public observatories.
- Science museums.

The platform should continue growing without losing its simplicity and accessibility.

---

# Final Statement

AstroAlert is designed for anyone who has ever looked up at the night sky and asked:

"What am I looking at?"

Every feature should help answer that question while inspiring the next one.
